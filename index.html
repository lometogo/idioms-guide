📘 Business English: Register & Word Choice

Real business writing from IBM — with context, register, and usage guidance
🎯 For Eloise: This page focuses on register — knowing whether an expression is formal, informal, neutral, or business jargon. Each footnote tells you when and where to use each word or phrase.
Source: IBM Think Leadership • August 13, 2026 • "Faster Not Better"

AI can make your business faster. That doesn't mean better.

For COOs, the real opportunity isn't using AI to speed up old workflows. It's using AI to redesign¹ work and reinvest² the gains in growth.

Most companies are using AI to do yesterday's work a little faster: improve productivity, reduce costs, streamline³ processes and make the business run better. Operational excellence¹⁵ has always been about helping the company move faster, smarter and more efficiently. But the organizations getting the most from AI aren't treating it solely as a cost reduction tool or another productivity program.

Every hour that AI gives back, every redundant task it eliminates⁴ and every decision it accelerates⁵ creates momentum for something bigger. Better customer experiences. New business models. New sources of growth. The operational improvements fund⁶ innovation. Innovation drives⁷ growth. Growth creates⁸ resources for the next round of transformation.

But, too often, companies apply AI to workflows that haven't been fundamentally examined⁹ in years. They use AI to generate reports that nobody reads. They automate approval chains that already involve too many people. They layer¹⁰ AI on top of processes that were already frustrating employees and customers. The result isn't transformation. It's just faster complexity.

"Stop optimizing what exists."¹⁶ — Yogi Goyal, IBM Consulting

Stop automating work that should disappear

First, eliminate⁴ process steps that no longer add value. Then automate the redesigned processes. Standardize what remains. Finally, reinvest² the gains into future growth.

Too many organizations automate what they should have eliminated. They put AI on top of broken workflows¹⁷ and end up scaling inefficiency¹¹. The sequence matters: eliminate the waste, automate the work that remains, standardize it across the enterprise and then reinvest the gains into innovation.

The hardest part of AI is EASY

1. Eliminate
The first step is deciding what work should disappear altogether. Too many companies start by layering AI onto existing processes. These inefficiencies survive simply because "that's the way it's always been done."¹² The real opportunity isn't to make old processes faster. It's to ask whether they would exist at all if you were designing the company today.

2. Automate
Once you've eliminated unnecessary work, then you move to automation. It's no longer just about automating repetitive tasks. AI can analyze thousands of customer interactions overnight, draft executive-ready summaries before you get to work, and surface¹³ operational risks before your company even recognizes a problem is emerging.

3. Standardize
AI won't scale in operational chaos. Enterprise-wide AI may be the goal, but in organizations where the same metric has five different definitions, customer data lives in a dozen systems and every department follows its own process, AI ends up trapped¹⁴ in isolated pilots. Consistent data structures, common terminology, shared governance¹⁸ and standardized workflows create the foundation that AI needs to operate across departmental boundaries.

4. Yield
The real question isn't how many hours AI saved — it's how much more capable is your organization.

📝 Footnotes

  1. redesign (ree-di-ZYNE) To design something again in a different way. ⚡ Register: Neutral / Professional Best used in: Business strategy discussions, project planning, reports. Avoid in: Casual conversation — it sounds too formal. Alternatives: rethink (less formal), revamp (informal), reconceptualize (more academic). Example: We need to redesign our customer onboarding process to reduce friction. ↩ back to text
  2. reinvest (ree-in-VEST) To put money or resources back into something to generate future returns. ⚡ Register: Formal / Business Best used in: Financial reports, board presentations, growth strategy meetings. Avoid in: Casual conversation — sounds too corporate. Alternatives: reallocate, plow back (informal). Example: We plan to reinvest our profits into R&D for new product lines. ↩ back to text
  3. streamline (STREEM-line) To make a process more efficient by removing unnecessary steps. ⚡ Register: Neutral / Professional Best used in: Operations meetings, process improvement discussions, internal emails. Avoid in: Casual conversation — it sounds like office jargon. Alternatives: simplify, make more efficient. Example: We need to streamline our approval process so projects move faster. ↩ back to text
  4. eliminate (ih-LIM-in-ayt) To completely remove or get rid of something. ⚡ Register: Neutral / Formal Best used in: Strategy documents, operational planning, improvement initiatives. Avoid in: Casual conversation — it can sound harsh or overly serious. Alternatives: cut (less formal), remove, phase out. Example: We should eliminate any steps in the process that don't add value for the customer. ↩ back to text
  5. accelerates (ak-SEL-er-ayts) To make something happen faster or more quickly. ⚡ Register: Neutral / Formal Best used in: Performance reviews, project updates, strategy discussions. Avoid in: Everyday conversation — it can sound like corporate buzzword. Alternatives: speeds up (informal), fast-tracks (business jargon). Example: Good data management accelerates our ability to make informed decisions. ↩ back to text
  6. fund (fuhnd) To provide money or resources for a project or initiative. ⚡ Register: Neutral / Business Best used in: Budget discussions, project proposals, financial reports. Avoid in: Casual conversation — sounds too financial. Alternatives: finance, support, pay for (informal). Example: The profits from our core business fund new product development. ↩ back to text
  7. drives (dryvz) To be the main force that causes something to happen or progress. ⚡ Register: Neutral / Business Best used in: Strategy discussions, marketing copy, internal communications. Avoid in: Overusing in formal writing — it's a business buzzword. Alternatives: fuels, powers, propels. Example: Customer satisfaction drives long-term business growth. ↩ back to text
  8. creates (kree-AYTS) To bring something into existence. ⚡ Register: Neutral (everyday) Best used in: Almost any context — formal, informal, spoken, written. Avoid in: No specific avoidance — this is a safe, universal word. Alternatives: generates, produces, builds. Example: A positive company culture creates a more productive work environment. ↩ back to text
  9. examined (eg-ZAM-ind) To have been inspected, analyzed, or reviewed carefully. ⚡ Register: Formal Best used in: Reports, audits, academic writing, official documents. Avoid in: Casual conversation — sounds too stiff or academic. Alternatives: looked at (informal), reviewed (neutral), analyzed (formal). Example: The board examined the financial records before approving the budget. ↩ back to text
  10. layer (as verb) (LAY-er) To add something on top of an existing thing, often making it more complex. ⚡ Register: Informal / Technical Best used in: IT discussions, process improvement conversations, internal team chats. Avoid in: Formal reports or client presentations — it's too casual. Alternatives: add, superimpose (formal). Example: We keep layering new software on top of old systems instead of replacing them. ↩ back to text
  11. scaling inefficiency (SKAYL-ing in-ih-FISH-en-see) When inefficient processes are expanded across an entire organization, making the problem bigger. ⚡ Register: Business jargon Best used in: Operations reviews, management consulting, strategy meetings. Avoid in: Casual conversation or external communications — it's insider jargon. Alternatives: spreading inefficiency, multiplying problems. Example: By automating broken processes, we're just scaling inefficiency across the company. ↩ back to text
  12. "that's the way it's always been done" (THATS thuh way its AL-weez been DUHN) A phrase used to justify continuing old habits or traditions, often without questioning them. ⚡ Register: Informal / Idiomatic Best used in: Internal conversations, meetings about change, team discussions. Avoid in: Formal documents, client presentations — it sounds too casual and unprofessional. Alternatives: business as usual, our existing process. Example: When I asked why we still do it, they said, "That's the way it's always been done." ↩ back to text
  13. surface (as verb) (SUR-fis) To bring something to attention; to make something visible that was previously hidden. ⚡ Register: Neutral / Professional Best used in: Business meetings, data analysis discussions, strategy sessions. Avoid in: Casual conversation — it's tech/business jargon. Alternatives: uncover, bring up (informal), highlight. Example: The new dashboard surfaces trends that we couldn't see before. ↩ back to text
  14. trapped (trapt) Stuck or unable to move forward; contained in a limited space. ⚡ Register: Neutral / Informal Best used in: Team discussions, internal emails, problem-solving conversations. Avoid in: Formal reports — sounds too emotional. Alternatives: stuck, confined, limited. Example: Our AI pilots are trapped in isolated departments and never scale company-wide. ↩ back to text
  15. operational excellence (op-er-AY-shun-al EK-sel-ens) A management philosophy focused on continuously improving operations and eliminating waste. ⚡ Register: Formal / Business jargon Best used in: Strategy documents, management meetings, consulting presentations. Avoid in: Casual conversation or external marketing — it's insider jargon. Alternatives: process excellence, operational efficiency. Example: Our operational excellence program helped us reduce costs by 15% last year. ↩ back to text
  16. "Stop optimizing what exists" (STOP OP-tim-ize-ing wut eg-ZISTS) A business mantra encouraging leaders to rethink existing processes rather than just improve them. ⚡ Register: Command phrase / Business mantra Best used in: Leadership communication, transformation initiatives, strategy meetings. Avoid in: Casual conversation — it's too directive and "management-speak." Alternatives: Don't just improve — reimagine, Challenge your assumptions. Example: Our CEO's message was clear: stop optimizing what exists and start building what's next. ↩ back to text
  17. broken workflows (BROH-ken WURK-flohz) Inefficient or dysfunctional processes that waste time and resources. ⚡ Register: Informal / Business Best used in: Internal team discussions, process improvement, operational reviews. Avoid in: Client communications or formal documents — sounds too blunt. Alternatives: inefficient processes, dysfunctional systems. Example: Before automating, we need to fix the broken workflows that are causing delays. ↩ back to text
  18. shared governance (SHAIRD GUV-er-nens) A system where decision-making authority is distributed among multiple stakeholders or departments. ⚡ Register: Formal / Business Best used in: Organizational design, leadership discussions, policy documents. Avoid in: Casual conversation — highly formal and academic-sounding. Alternatives: collaborative decision-making, distributed authority. Example: We need shared governance to ensure all departments have input on data standards. ↩ back to text
