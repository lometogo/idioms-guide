📖 Idioms & Special Phrases — Plain-English Guide

Written for a high school freshman. No jargon, no fluff.

"the lead engineer" metaphor
This just means the main AI acts like a boss who hands out tasks. It's not a real idiom, just a way to make the tech sound more human. The main AI runs the whole project, and subagents are like specialists it brings in when it needs help with something specific.
"pulls in" phrasal verb
Means brings in temporarily. Like when a teacher asks a student who's good at math to help the group for a few minutes, then that student goes back to their own work. Subagents are only around for one job, then they're done.
"starts fresh" idiom
Means begins with nothing from before — like a blank sheet of paper. The subagent doesn't carry over all the chat history from the main agent, so it doesn't waste memory on stuff that doesn't matter to its one task.
"fills up with" metaphor
Means gets totally loaded with. Think of your phone's storage — if you save too many photos, it fills up and you can't take more. Same idea here: if the main agent kept all the subagent's work, its memory would get clogged.
"hunts security holes" metaphor
Means looks really carefully for weak spots in the code. Like a detective searching for clues. "Holes" are vulnerabilities that hackers could sneak through. The reviewer subagent's whole job is to find them before anyone else does.
"just enough shell access" principle phrase
Means the absolute minimum permission needed — and nothing extra. Like giving someone a key to the supply closet but not to the main office. The test runner can run tests, but it can't delete files or change settings it shouldn't touch.
"That's the conceptual floor" metaphor
Means this is the very basic starting point. Think of a building — the floor is where you stand to begin with. The author is saying, "This is the minimum you need to know before we go further up (or deeper) into the details."
"goes deeper on the why" common phrase
Means explains the reasons more thoroughly. "The why" is just shorthand for the purpose or reasoning behind something. It's the difference between telling you what to do and telling you why it matters.
"the rest of this guide is about building them" roadmap phrase
Not really an idiom — it's just the author telling you what's coming next. Think of it as a signpost that says, "Now we move from theory to hands-on stuff."
"applied at the level of individual tasks" technical phrasing
Means used for each single, separate job. The same basic setup (an AI with its own instructions and tools) that works for the main agent is also used for each subagent, but scaled down to just one specific job at a time.
